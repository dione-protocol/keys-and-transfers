## Why this question keeps coming up

Coinbase is the default reference point for people entering crypto, so "is it on Coinbase" becomes shorthand for "is it real and can I buy it easily". Those are two different questions, and the second one has a broader answer than the first.

A project can be entirely real and unlisted on any particular exchange. Conversely, a listing says a venue will trade something, which is a commercial decision rather than an endorsement.

## Can you buy DIONE with a bank card?

On venues that support card purchases, yes - usually through a payment processor, with a fee on top of the exchange's own. The card route is the fastest and the most expensive; a bank transfer is slower and cheaper.

Three things to know before using a card:

- **The processor's fee is separate** from the trading fee, and it is often the larger of the two.
- **Some banks block crypto purchases** by default, which appears as a declined transaction rather than an error message about crypto.
- **The quoted rate includes the spread**, so compare the total against a second venue if the amount is significant.

What a card purchase does not do is put DIONE in your own wallet. It creates a balance on the exchange, which is custody until you withdraw.

## Does buying DIONE require KYC?

On regulated exchanges, generally yes: an account, identity documents and whatever checks the venue's jurisdiction requires. That is the venue's obligation, not the chain's.

On a decentralised venue, no - you connect a wallet and swap. What you take on instead is the responsibility for the contract address, the network and the gas, none of which an exchange handles for you.

DIONE Wallet itself performs no identity verification and collects no personal data - the wallet is software on your machine, and there is no account to verify. That is a property of self-custody rather than a policy choice.

## Was DIONE delisted from any exchange?

The project's material does not record a delisting. That is the honest limit of what an article can say: listing status is each venue's decision and can change without notice, and a historical record here would be out of date the moment it was written.

What you can check: whether the pair trades now, at the venue you intend to use. If a pair has gone, the asset still exists on-chain and remains transferable; a delisting affects where you can trade, not whether you own anything.

## The custody question behind the question

Most people asking about a specific exchange are really asking whether it is safe to buy there. The relevant distinction is custody:

**On an exchange**, your balance is a claim on the venue. It is convenient, it is tradeable, and it depends on the venue remaining solvent and willing to honour withdrawals.

**In your own wallet**, the balance is yours, backed by a key only you hold. It is less convenient and it cannot be frozen by a third party - but a lost key is unrecoverable.

Neither is universally right. A common arrangement is a trading balance on an exchange and the rest in self-custody, and the split is a risk decision. [Is DIONE Wallet Safe? Audit, Signing and Self-Custody](https://github.com/dione-protocol/keys-and-transfers/blob/main/is-dione-wallet-safe.md) covers the wallet's side of it.

## If you buy and want to withdraw

Two details decide whether the withdrawal arrives:

**The network.** Native DIONE goes to Odyssey Chain; ERC-20 goes to Ethereum. A withdrawal sent on the wrong network is not something the exchange can reverse.

**The address.** Check it character by character, and send a small test amount the first time. [Create a New DIONE Wallet: First Run Walkthrough](https://github.com/dione-protocol/setup-and-troubleshooting/blob/main/create-a-new-dione-wallet.md) covers preparing the destination, and [DIONE Migration Verifier Shows No Balance](https://github.com/dione-protocol/keys-and-transfers/blob/main/dione-migration-verifier-shows-no-balance.md) the check when a balance does not show up.

## What to do if Coinbase is where you want to buy

Two honest options: buy elsewhere and withdraw, or wait and check whether the venue lists it. Buying a different asset and swapping adds a step and usually a cost, and it is not equivalent to buying DIONE.

What does not work is assuming a listing because a tracking site shows a ticker. Aggregators list markets, and a market existing somewhere is not a venue you can use.
